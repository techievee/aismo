Privacy policy — aismo (AI SMS Manager)

Last updated 21 November 2026 · aismo for Android, package aismo.sms

The short version: everything stays on your phone. aismo does not hold Android's internet permission, so it cannot send your messages, contacts or anything else to any server. It has no account, no ads and no usage statistics, and it sends nothing anywhere, ever.

What the app does

aismo shows your text and picture messages, files them into categories (one-time codes, bank alerts, bills, orders, travel, promotions and your own categories), totals your spending, searches your messages by words or by meaning, and can rewrite your drafts. All of this — including the AI — runs on the phone. Nothing is processed in the cloud.

What aismo reads, and why

  • Your messages (SMS and MMS). With your permission, aismo reads your conversations so it can show, sort, search, total and back them up. They are stored in a private database on your phone and never leave it.
  • Your contacts. So it can show names instead of numbers and suggest contacts as you type. Contacts are never copied anywhere; aismo keeps no copy of them.
  • Which SIM your phone has. So you can choose which SIM a message goes out on. The number itself is only shown to you, in the SIM picker.

What aismo never does

  • It has no internet permission, so it cannot contact any server, show ads, or report anything. One library it uses (ONNX Runtime) requests internet access and includes a client that reports usage; both were removed from the app, and a test checks the installed app on every run.
  • It keeps no account and collects no usage statistics.
  • It does not share your data with anyone, including the developer. The only exception by nature of the platform: picture messages travel through your carrier's standard MMS service, exactly as in any messaging app, and messages sent and received use your carrier's normal SMS service.

Backups

Backups are something you start, not something that happens. When you create one, it is encrypted with a passphrase you choose and saved to a location you choose; aismo cannot read it without that passphrase, and the backup only goes where you put it.

Permissions

aismo asks only for what it needs to do its job: read, send and store your messages (Android allows this only to the default SMS app), show notifications, read your contacts, know your SIMs, vibrate, restart its background jobs after a phone restart, and ask for your fingerprint or the app's PIN when you have locked a category. The app's in-app help screen lists every permission it holds, and why.

Changes and questions

Changes to this policy are published with the app's source code. Questions about this policy can be sent to the developer through the contact details listed with the app on Google Play.

aismo is available at github.com/techievee/aismo and on Google Play.
